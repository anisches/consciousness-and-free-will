# Neuroscience and Agency

This folder tracks neuroscience that bears on agency, volition, action awareness, and unity of consciousness. It should pressure philosophical claims, not replace them.

## Main Questions

- When does the brain prepare an action relative to conscious intention?
- Is conscious intention causal, interpretive, supervisory, veto-like, or retrospective?
- How does the sense of agency arise?
- What happens when action control, action ownership, and action explanation come apart?
- Which findings matter for moral responsibility, and which only matter for a narrow model of conscious initiation?

## Source Clusters

### Animal Experiments Relevant to Agency

- [Animal experiments relevant to agency](animal-agency-experiments.md)

Use this cluster for non-human evidence about self-initiated action timing, evidence accumulation, goal-directed versus habitual action, deliberation, and causal circuit manipulation. Keep the claim narrow: animal experiments explain mechanisms beneath agency; they do not settle metaphysical free will by themselves.

### Readiness Potential

- Libet et al., 1983: https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/106.3.623
- Schurger et al., 2012: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1210467109

Track the difference between "brain activity precedes report" and "the decision was fully made unconsciously." Those are not the same claim.

### Predictive Decoding

- Soon et al., 2008: https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2112

Questions to preserve:

- What exactly was decoded?
- How accurate was prediction?
- Was the task morally or practically meaningful?
- Does prediction imply determination, bias, preparation, or only weak statistical information?

### Sense of Agency

- Haggard, 2017: https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2017.14
- Wegner and Wheatley, 1999: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10424155/
- Moore and Obhi, 2012: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00179

Core distinction: agency can mean causing an action, experiencing oneself as causing it, explaining it afterward, or being morally responsible for it.

### Split Brain and Confabulation

- Sperry, 1968: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0026839
- Gazzaniga, 2015: https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2015.8

Use this cluster to test assumptions about unified consciousness, unified agency, and narrative explanation.

### Disorders of Volition

- Frith, Blakemore, and Wolpert, 2000: https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2000.0734
- Biran and Chatterjee, 2004: https://doi.org/10.1001/archneur.61.2.292

Clinical cases to track:

- Alien hand syndrome
- Anosognosia
- Utilization behavior
- Compulsions
- Schizophrenia-related agency disturbance

## Caution

Neuroscience can pressure naive accounts of conscious will, but it does not automatically settle metaphysical free will or moral responsibility. Keep these levels separate:

- timing of neural preparation
- conscious report of intention
- action ownership
- action control
- reasons-responsiveness
- moral responsibility
- blame and punishment
