# Quantum Indeterminacy, Decoherence, and Agency

## Core thesis

Quantum mechanics introduces indeterminacy into physical theory, but indeterminacy alone is not free will. A random quantum event is not an authored choice. For quantum physics to matter to agency, it must be integrated into a control architecture that explains reasons, feedback, inhibition, and responsibility.

## Main lines

- Collapse or measurement randomness can break classical determinism.
- Decoherence explains why macroscopic systems often behave classically.
- Quantum mind theories such as Orch-OR try to connect quantum events to consciousness, but remain controversial.
- Even if quantum noise enters the brain, noise must be shaped by organism-level control to become agency-relevant.

## Counterpoint

The "randomness rescue" fails if it merely replaces deterministic compulsion with stochastic compulsion. Freedom requires controlled responsiveness to reasons, not dice inside neurons.
