# Impact of Unsegregated Synapses and Non-Spiking Neurons on Functional Connectivity
A new quantification method for pre- and post-synaptic segregation reveals that many neurons in the *Drosophila* brain possess unsegregated synapses. These highly unsegregated synapses, characteristic of non-spiking neurons, contribute significantly more to functional connectivity (FC) than highly segregated synapses.

These non-spiking neurons are distributed throughout the whole brain and exert a substantial influence on global FC. This challenges the spike-centric view of structural-functional relationships in connectomics and highlights the importance of graded potentials and unsegregated synaptic architectures in shaping brain-wide activity patterns.
