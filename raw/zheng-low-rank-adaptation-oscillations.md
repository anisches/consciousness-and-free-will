# Mean-field Theory of Low-rank Recurrent Networks with Adaptation

- **Source**: [[2606.30366.md]] (Zheng, Miller, & Fiete, 2026)
- **Topics**: [[neural-manifolds]], [[chaos]], [[adaptation]], [[mean-field-theory]]

## Summary

Zheng et al. (2026) developed a dynamical mean-field theory for random recurrent networks that combine **low-rank structure** (defining a coherent mode) with **activity-dependent adaptation**. 

The interaction between the random connectivity (which generates chaos) and the low-rank structure (which enables coherence) creates a rich repertoire of dynamics. As adaptation strength increases, the system transitions through distinct regimes, identifying how single-neuron adaptation parameters shape global network states.

## Key Findings

- **Regime Transition**: Increasing adaptation drives the network from static states to noise-sustained oscillations, stochastic switching, and eventually global limit cycles.
- **Two Instabilities**: The theory reconciles two mechanisms:
    1. **Chaos onset**: Driven by the random connectivity component ($g$).
    2. **Hopf bifurcation**: Driven by the coherent mode and adaptation.
- **Frequency-Dependent Transfer**: Adaptation acts as a frequency-dependent filter in the single-neuron transfer function, which determines which instability wins.

## Relation to Agency

This provides a mathematical bridge between the "deterministic chaos" of random networks and the "structured agency" of low-rank, goal-directed networks. It suggests that agency (coherent action) is not a separate thing from chaos but a mode that can be "tuned" by biological adaptation (e.g., neuromodulation, fatigue).
