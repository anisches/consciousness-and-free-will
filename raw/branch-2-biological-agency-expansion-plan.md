# Biological Agency Expansion Plan: Sapolsky, Mitchell, and Animal Models

Scope: deepen `biology/README.md` around hard determinism, organismic agency, stress physiology, development, animal decision-making, and ecological feedback.

## Key claims to add

1. **Sapolsky's strongest case is multi-scale causality, not crude genetic fatalism.** Actions are shaped by neural events, hormones, stress, development, culture, evolution, and random biological contingency.
2. **Biological constraint is unevenly distributed.** Trauma, poverty, hunger, sleep loss, social rank, and chronic stress alter prefrontal control, threat sensitivity, reward valuation, and impulse regulation before conscious deliberation starts.
3. **Mitchell's counter-case is organization.** Living systems are not passive particles: they maintain themselves, model environments, pursue goals, and use higher-level organismic states to constrain lower-level parts.
4. **Animal studies make agency experimentally tractable.** Goal-directed action, habit, outcome devaluation, self-initiated movement, and hippocampal deliberation show proto-agency without relying on human introspection.
5. **The hard problem for both sides is responsibility.** Sapolsky explains why blame is biologically naive; Mitchell explains why reasons and control are not illusions. A mature branch should hold both claims in tension.

## Critiques / counterpoints to include

- **Biological causation does not automatically erase agency.** It may explain how agency is implemented rather than proving it is unreal.
- **Top-down causation must avoid hand-waving.** A higher-level state is causally useful only if it identifies real constraints, control variables, or intervention points.
- **Animal agency is not human moral responsibility.** Goal-directed behavior and planning are necessary ingredients, not the full human legal/moral package.
- **Stress explanations can be politically important but epistemically dangerous.** They can support compassion and institutional reform without collapsing all differences in control into zero control.
