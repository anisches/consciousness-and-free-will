# Gödel, Mind, Penrose, and the Feferman Critique

## Core thesis

Lucas and Penrose argue that human mathematical understanding cannot be captured by any fixed formal machine, because a human can allegedly see the truth of a Gödel sentence the machine cannot prove. This is one of the strongest anti-mechanist hooks in the free-will/consciousness debate.

## The critique

Feferman and others argue that the move depends on assumptions about human soundness and consistency. A human can only assert the Gödel sentence's truth if they know the target system is sound; but humans are fallible, inconsistent, and not transparently formalizable as a single stable proof system.

## Why it matters for agency

The branch is useful not because it settles free will, but because it asks whether minds are fixed algorithms, changing meta-systems, probabilistic reasoners, or something non-computable.
