# The Free Will Theorem and Superdeterminism

## Core thesis

Foundations of quantum mechanics often depend on assumptions about measurement independence: experimenters can choose settings not fully correlated with the hidden variables being measured. Conway and Kochen's Free Will Theorem dramatizes this by saying, roughly, that if experimenters have a certain freedom, then particle responses are not functions of prior accessible information. Superdeterminism closes this door by denying measurement independence.

## Why it matters

- Physics imports assumptions about experimental freedom and causal independence.
- The Free Will Theorem is not a proof of human moral free will.
- Superdeterminism preserves determinism but makes the status of interventions and experiments philosophically expensive.

## Counterpoint

The branch is about the metaphysics of physical law, not everyday agency. It should not be oversold as "physics proves free will."
