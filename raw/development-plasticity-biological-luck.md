# Development, Plasticity, and Biological Luck

## Core thesis

The chooser is built before it chooses. Early life, attachment, nutrition, social status, education, trauma, epigenetic regulation, and plasticity alter later temperament, impulse control, fear learning, and cognitive style. Sapolsky's "luck" argument lives here: no one chooses the developmental conditions that construct their choosing apparatus.

## Why it matters

- Moral and legal systems often assume a standardized agent.
- Developmental biology shows the agent is historically sculpted.
- Plasticity prevents fatalism: causes can be changed by therapy, education, environment, and institutions.

## Counterpoint

Developmental influence is not destiny. Plasticity means histories constrain but also remain open to intervention.
