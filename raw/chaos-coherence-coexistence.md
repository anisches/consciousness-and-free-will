# Coexistence of Population Coherence and Single-Neuron Chaos

- **Source**: [[zheng-low-rank-adaptation-oscillations.md]]
- **Topics**: [[neural-manifolds]], [[chaos]], [[stochasticity]], [[coherence]]

## The Phenomenon

A key result of the mean-field theory by Zheng et al. (2026) is the **coexistence** of coherent population-level oscillations with heterogeneous firing rates and network-generated stochasticity at the single-neuron level.

## Mechanism

- **Population Level**: The low-rank component of the weight matrix organizes a global mode that oscillates rhythmically.
- **Single-Neuron Level**: The random component of the weight matrix ($g$) creates a "bath" of chaotic activity.

Crucially, the "noise" seen by an individual neuron is not external (like synaptic noise) but is **network-generated stochasticity**. The population oscillation "rides" on top of this chaotic background without suppressing it.

## Implication for Agency

This provides a mathematical model for how "intent" (the coherent mode) can exist alongside "noise" or "determinism" (the chaotic background). It suggests that the "determined" micro-states (chaos) and "agentic" macro-states (coherence) are not mutually exclusive but are coupled through the network's rank structure.
