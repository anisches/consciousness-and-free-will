# Animal Agency: Goal-Directed Action, Habit, and Deliberation

## Core thesis

Animal experiments make agency operational. Researchers can separate goal-directed action from habit, track self-initiated movement, observe hippocampal look-ahead at decision points, and intervene in basal-ganglia circuits to bias action selection.

## Key mechanisms

- **Outcome devaluation:** if an animal stops pursuing a devalued reward, action is goal-directed rather than mere habit.
- **Vicarious trial and error:** hippocampal activity can represent possible future paths at choice points.
- **Readiness and accumulation:** action timing can arise from neural population dynamics.
- **Basal ganglia control:** circuit manipulations can bias go/no-go action selection.
- **Homeostatic robustness:** neural circuits (e.g. crab pyloric system) maintain goal-directed output across environmental perturbations like temperature via overlapping compensatory mechanisms. [[raw/marder-crab-thermal-compensation-homeostasis.md]]

## Why it matters

Agency is not all-or-nothing. It appears in layers: self-initiation, goal-sensitivity, future simulation, inhibition, and model-based control.
