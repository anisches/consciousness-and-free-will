# Reading Lists

These are routes through the source map, not final syllabi. The first job is to build enough coverage that later notes can compare claims instead of reacting to isolated famous examples.

## Path 1: Orientation Before Commitments

1. SEP, "Consciousness": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness/
2. SEP, "Free Will": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freewill/
3. SEP, "Moral Responsibility": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/
4. IEP, "The Hard Problem of Consciousness": https://iep.utm.edu/hard-problem-of-conciousness/
5. IEP, "Free Will": https://iep.utm.edu/freewill/

Use this path to build vocabulary and avoid mixing up consciousness, agency, responsibility, and blame.

## Path 3: Free Will and Responsibility

1. Aristotle, _Nicomachean Ethics_, Book III
2. Hume, _Enquiry_, Section VIII
3. Frankfurt, "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility"
4. Strawson, "Freedom and Resentment"
5. van Inwagen, _An Essay on Free Will_
6. Fischer and Ravizza, _Responsibility and Control_
7. Kane, _The Significance of Free Will_
8. Pereboom, _Living Without Free Will_

Track whether each source is talking about alternative possibilities, sourcehood, reasons-responsiveness, desert, punishment, or practical agency.

## Path 4: Neuroscience of Agency and Volition

1. Libet et al., "Time of Conscious Intention to Act in Relation to Onset of Cerebral Activity"
2. Soon et al., "Unconscious Determinants of Free Decisions in the Human Brain"
3. Schurger et al., "An Accumulator Model for Spontaneous Neural Activity Prior to Self-Initiated Movement"
4. Haggard, "Sense of Agency in the Human Brain"
5. Wegner and Wheatley, "Apparent Mental Causation"
6. Moore and Obhi, "Intentional Binding and the Sense of Agency"
7. Sperry, "Hemisphere Deconnection and Unity in Conscious Awareness"
8. Gazzaniga, "Forty-Five Years of Split-Brain Research and Still Going Strong"
9. Frith, Blakemore, and Wolpert, "Abnormalities in the Awareness and Control of Action"
10. Biran and Chatterjee, "Alien Hand Syndrome"
11. Jacquerie & Marder (2026), "Compensation of the effects of temperature on a motor system in the crab"
12. Yu et al. (Hafed lab) (2026), "Much stronger coarse-to-fine visual processing in primate superior colliculus"
13. Wickramanayaka & Oizumi (2026), "Representational alignment of color using GWOT"
14. Huang et al. (2026), "Cx26 hemichannels mediate hypercapnic arousal"
15. Sun et al. (2026), "Scanning and active sampling emerge from conserved insect neural circuits"

Read this path with caution: neuroscience can challenge naive stories about conscious initiation, but it does not by itself settle metaphysical free will or moral responsibility.
