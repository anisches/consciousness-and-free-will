## Idea
# One operator to rule them all: Unifying connectome harmonics, turbulence and complex harmonics in brain dynamics (2026)

**Authors**: Morten L. Kringelbach, Gustavo Deco
**Keywords**: Connectome Laplacian, Functional Calculus, Turbulence, CHARM, Spectral Gap, LSD

## Overview

Kringelbach and Deco (2026) provide a rigorous unification of three dominant frameworks for brain dynamics: **Connectome Harmonics**, **Turbulence**, and **Complex Harmonics (CHARM)**. They demonstrate that these are not merely complementary descriptions but different mathematical facets of a single self-adjoint operator—the **Connectome Laplacian** ($L$).

## The Unifying Identity

The unification is framed within the **functional calculus** of the Laplacian. Each framework corresponds to a specific function of the same operator:

1. **Connectome Harmonics** ($\phi$): The spectral projections (eigenfunctions) of $L$.
2. **Turbulence** ($\mathcal{K}$): The resolvent of $L$, expressed through the Green's function of a screened Laplacian: $\mathcal{K} = (L + \alpha I)^{-1}$. This links the empirical "exponential distance rule" of turbulence directly to the Laplacian.
3. **CHARM** ($\mathcal{U}$): The unitary propagator $e^{iLt}$.

## The Spectral Gap as a Universal Controller

The **spectral gap** of the Laplacian emerges as the single shared control parameter that simultaneously determines the depth of the cortical hierarchy, the rate of the turbulent information cascade, and the structured interference patterns of complex harmonics.

## Empirical Validation: LSD Perturbation

The theory makes a strong predictive claim: a structural perturbation to the operator must shift all three functional signatures in unison with a single coupling parameter.

The authors tested this by perturbing the Connectome Laplacian using a **5-HT2A receptor density map** (the primary site of action for LSD). They found that a single scalar coupling could simultaneously predict:
- The multi-scale shift in turbulence observed through the resolvent.
- The macroscale redistribution of harmonic energy observed through first-order Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory.

## Implications for Agency

This unification provides a rigorous geometric basis for understanding how structural constraints (the connectome and receptor landscapes) dictate the range of possible functional states. By reducing disparate dynamical signatures to a single "ruling operator," it suggests that the "shape" of consciousness and the "landscape" of choice are governed by a unified spectral law.
