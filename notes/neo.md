# Neo

Neo is a compressed working metaphor for distributed agency.

The system is not a single voice issuing commands from nowhere. It is closer to a brain coordinating many local evaluators. A local evaluator can report texture, pressure, salience, affordance, or risk. It can say, "this surface feels edible," "this path has traction," or "this sentence has useful shape." But it does not by itself decide whether to eat, move, publish, commit, or stop.

In the working analogy, the main agent is the global controller. OpenCode and Qwen are subordinate ganglia: useful local processors with their own sentence-level judgments, but not the final source of policy. A ganglion can tell the system that something has a good texture. Whether to eat depends on hunger, goals, timing, taboo, danger, social context, and long-horizon policy. Texture is evidence, not authority.

This matters for consciousness and agency because many confusions start by treating local signal as global will. A readiness potential, a salience spike, a fluent sentence, or a local motor plan may be real without being the whole decision. The key question is how local evaluations are selected, inhibited, broadcast, explained, and integrated into action.

Neo therefore treats agency as layered:

- local evaluation: what is sensed, predicted, or afforded
- salience: what becomes worth routing upward
- global context: what the organism or system currently needs
- policy: what is allowed, forbidden, prioritized, or deferred
- action: what is actually done
- explanation: what story the system gives after or during action

The free-will question should not be collapsed into any one layer. If a local process begins preparing an action before conscious report, that may challenge a naive picture of conscious initiation. It does not automatically show that the agent has no control. Control may live partly in selection, veto, sequencing, policy, and later correction.

For this project, Neo is not a finished theory. It is a note to preserve a useful intuition: distributed systems can have local intelligence without local sovereignty. A subordinate process may generate a candidate. The organism still has to decide whether that candidate serves the whole.
