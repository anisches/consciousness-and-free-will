# The Free Will Theorem and Particle Agency

The **Free Will Theorem**, proposed by John Conway and Simon Kochen, establishes a conditional link between the "free will" of experimenters and the "free will" of elementary particles.

## The Theorem
If the choice of measurement directions by an experimenter is not a function of the past history of the universe (i.e., they have "free will"), then the response of the particle being measured is also not a function of the past history of the universe.

## Formal Statement
Assuming three axioms:
1. **[[20260704120001-spin-axiom-101-property]]**
2. **[[20260704120005-epr-paradox-twin-axiom]]**
3. **[[20260704120004-causality-and-the-light-speed-barrier]]**

If humans have the freedom to choose their experimental setup, then particles must have the same level of freedom in choosing their responses.

## "Particle Free Will"
Conway is careful to define "free will" for particles as **indeterminacy** relative to the past. It does not imply that particles have minds, consciousness, or "intentions." It simply means that their behavior is not "law-like" in a deterministic sense. The particle's response is a "new" piece of information in the universe, not a calculation based on previous states.

## Philosophical Impact
The theorem challenges biological determinism and suggests that the universe is fundamentally non-deterministic at the smallest scales. It shifts the debate from "whether we have free will" to "if we have it, so does everything else."
