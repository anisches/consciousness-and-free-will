# The EPR Paradox and the Twin Axiom

The **Twin Axiom** in the Free Will Theorem is derived from the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) experiment and the phenomenon of quantum entanglement.

## The Twin Axiom
If two spin-1 particles are prepared in a "twinned" state (a specific entangled state with total angular momentum zero) and then separated, they will always give the same response ($0$ or $1$) if the same measurement direction is queried on both.

## The EPR Argument
Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen (1935) argued that if you can predict the value of a property of a particle (like spin) by measuring a distant "twin" particle without disturbing the first, then that property must be an "element of reality" (i.e., it must have existed before measurement).

However, quantum mechanics predicts these correlations even though the values are not determined beforehand.

## Role in the Theorem
The Twin Axiom allows the "experimenters" in the theorem (Alice and Bob) to perform measurements on separated particles while knowing that the particles' "internal states" (if any) are correlated. This setup, combined with the [[20260704120002-kochen-specker-paradox-indeterminacy]], forces the conclusion that the particle's response is not a function of the past.
