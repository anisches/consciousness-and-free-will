# The Kochen-Specker Paradox and Indeterminacy

The **Kochen-Specker Paradox** (1967) demonstrates that quantum mechanics is incompatible with "non-contextual" hidden variable theories.

## The Paradox
The paradox uses a configuration of directions (such as the 33 directions in the original Kochen-Specker proof or the 18-direction Peres configuration) to show that it is mathematically impossible to assign a definite value ($0$ or $1$) to every direction in space such that:
1. Every direction has a fixed value regardless of which orthogonal triplet it belongs to.
2. Every orthogonal triplet of directions contains exactly two $1$s and one $0$ (the [[20260704120001-spin-axiom-101-property]]).

## Philosophical Conclusion
Because no such assignment exists, the squared spin component cannot exist as a fixed property of the particle (a function of direction) before it is measured.

Conway emphasizes that the spin value is not merely "unknown" but "non-existent" until the measurement occurs. This is a form of **indeterminacy**: the particle's response is not pre-determined by its internal state or the direction of the measurement alone.

## Role in Free Will Theorem
In the Free Will Theorem, this paradox is used to show that if the axioms hold, the particle's response cannot be a function of the past history of the universe.
