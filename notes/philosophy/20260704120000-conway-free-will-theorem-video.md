## Summary
In this lecture, John Conway presents the formal proof of the "Free Will Theorem," which he developed with Simon Kochen. The theorem asserts that if experimenters have free will (meaning their choices are not determined by the past), then the particles they measure must also possess a similar form of free will.

## Key Concepts
- **SPIN Axiom (101 Law)**: Measuring squared spin in three orthogonal directions always yields two 1s and one 0.
- **TWIN Axiom**: Entangled particles in a singleton state give the same answers for the same questions.
- **FIN Axiom**: Information cannot travel faster than light; specifically, choice at Earth cannot affect a particle on Mars instantly.
- **Kochen-Specker Paradox**: Proves the impossibility of a pre-determined function of direction that satisfies the SPIN axiom.

## The Proof Structure
1. **Assumption**: Assume the particle's response is a function of the past universe history ($\alpha, \beta$).
2. **Refinement**: Show that any "bad" bits (bits depending on choices) can be replaced by "good" bits (pre-determined values for all possible choices).
3. **Contradiction**: Under the axioms (SPIN, TWIN, FIN), the particle's response reduces to a function of a single direction that satisfies the 101 law. Since the Kochen-Specker paradox proves such a function cannot exist, the initial assumption of functional response is false.
