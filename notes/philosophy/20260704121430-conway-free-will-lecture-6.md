# Literature Note: Conway Free Will Lecture 6 - Philosophical Consequences

**Video ID:** IgvkhgE1Cps
**Series:** Free Will Theorem
**Speaker:** John Conway
**Topic:** Philosophical consequences and relationships of the Free Will Theorem.

## Summary
In this final lecture of the series, Conway explores why it is "okay to believe in free will" and why determinism lacks evidence despite its consistency. He argues that while determinism cannot be deductively disproved, it offers no explanatory power. In contrast, assuming human free will leads to a consistent explanation for quantum indeterminacy and the origin of our own agency.

## Key Arguments
- **Consistency vs. Truth:** Determinism is consistent (the "second time around" argument) but consistency doesn't guarantee truth (e.g., non-Euclidean geometry).
- **The "Surprised" Argument:** Assuming human free will explains why quantum mechanics is incomplete. This "surprise" turning out to be true provides evidence for the premise.
- **Ineluctable Concepts:** Fundamental concepts like probability and free will cannot be defined in simpler terms without assuming them.
- **Absence of Evidence for Determinism:** Newtonian determinism was successful but was superseded by quantum mechanics. Modern belief in determinism is often a "necessary illusion" without physical backing.
- **General Relativity Compatibility:** The shape of space-time (distribution of mass) can be seen as a result of free choices rather than a pre-determined structure.
- **Time Reversibility:** While Newtonian mechanics is time-reversible, the real world (and wave function collapse) is not.
