# Axioms of the Free Will Theorem

The **Free Will Theorem**, proposed by John Conway and Simon Kochen, rests on three fundamental axioms. These axioms are used to prove that if experimenters have "free will" (meaning their choices are not a function of the past), then particles must also have "free will."

## 1. SPIN Axiom
This is the [[20260704120100-kochen-specker-theorem-101-property|101 Property]] of spin-1 particles. It states that squared spin measurements in three orthogonal directions always yield two 1s and one 0.

## 2. FIN Axiom
Information cannot travel faster than a certain finite speed ($c$, the speed of light). This ensures that an experimenter's choice in one region of spacetime cannot instantaneously affect a particle in a distant region (preserving locality).

## 3. TWIN Axiom
It is possible to "entangle" two spin-1 particles such that they are in a "singleton state." In this state, if the same squared spin measurement is performed on both particles in the same direction, they will always give the same result (0 or 1), regardless of their distance.

## Conclusion of the Theorem
From these axioms, Conway and Kochen deduce that the particle's response cannot be a function of the past history of the universe. If it were, and if experimenters had freedom of choice, a contradiction would arise (based on the geometry of the [[20260704120500-kochen-specker-33-direction-proof|33 directions]]).
