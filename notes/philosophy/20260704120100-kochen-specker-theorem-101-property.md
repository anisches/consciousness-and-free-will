# Kochen-Specker Theorem and the 101 Property

The **Kochen-Specker theorem** (1967) demonstrates that quantum mechanics is "contextual." Specifically, it proves that it is impossible to assign predetermined values (0 or 1) to the squared spin components of a spin-1 particle for all possible directions in a way that is consistent with the **101 Property** (also called the **Spin Axiom**).

## The 101 Property (Spin Axiom)
For a spin-1 particle, if you measure the squared component of spin ($S^2$) in three mutually perpendicular (orthogonal) directions, the results will always be (1, 0, 1) in some order. That is:
- One direction will result in 0.
- The other two directions will result in 1.

## Significance
This property implies that the result of a measurement in one direction depends on the "context" of the other two directions it is measured with (even though the measurements commute). It ruled out "non-contextual hidden variable theories," showing that the particle's state for a given direction cannot be said to "exist" independently of the measurement context.

## Relation to Free Will
Conway and Kochen use this as the foundation for the [[20260704120200-conway-kochen-free-will-theorem-axioms|Free Will Theorem]], arguing that if the result is not predetermined, the particle has a degree of "freedom" or indeterminacy.
