# The Janus Model and Symmetry Violation

The **Janus Model** is a thought experiment used by John Conway to demonstrate how a physical theory can be logically consistent but physically invalid due to a violation of symmetry.

## The Model
Named after the two-faced Roman god Janus, the model assumes:
- An "operator" of the universe who exists "outside" the model.
- This operator can observe the entire state of the universe across all time and space.
- To maintain consistency between entangled particles (TWIN axiom) and the speed of light limit (FIN axiom), Janus can simply fill in the answers to experiments in a specific time order that he chooses.

## The Violation of Relativistic Invariance
While the Janus model can successfully simulate the results of quantum experiments, it does so by selecting an **arbitrary frame of reference** (a specific time ordering). 
- In the "Hexagonal Universe" toy model, Janus might work from right to left or left to right.
- In our universe, this corresponds to choosing a specific Lorentzian frame.
- However, the physical facts of our universe are **relativistically invariant** (they have the same form in all inertial frames).

## Failure of Curie's Principle
Because the Janus model requires a specific, non-symmetric mechanism to explain symmetric facts, it violates **Curie's Principle**. A theory that is not invariant under the same symmetries as the facts it explains cannot be considered the true explanation.

Conway uses this to argue against "cheap" explanations (like certain hidden variable theories or solipsism) that maintain consistency at the cost of the universe's fundamental symmetries.
