# The Free Will Theorem Statement

The **Free Will Theorem**, formulated by John Conway and Simon Kochen, states that:

> If the choice of directions in which to perform an experiment is free, then the response of the particle is similarly free.

In this context, "free" is a technical term meaning that the event (either the experimenter's choice or the particle's response) is not a mathematical function of the entire past history of the universe in any given coordinate frame.

The theorem implies that if we (humans) have free will, then elementary particles (like spin-1 particles) must also possess a form of free will. Conversely, if particles have no free will (i.e., they are deterministic), then we have none either.

## Philosophical Implications
This theorem suggests that indeterminism is not just a property of our ignorance, but a fundamental property of the universe if we assume the existence of human free agency. It challenges the "hidden variable" theories that seek to restore determinism to quantum mechanics.
