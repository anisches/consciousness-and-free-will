# Lorentzian Symmetry and Hyperbolic Spacetime

John Conway presents a simplified geometric model of Special Relativity to explain why the speed of light is constant for all observers.

## Hyperbolic Model
Instead of the classical Galilean view where time is absolute (straight lines $X+Y = 2T$), relativity suggests a model where the "ticks" of a clock are defined by hyperbolas:
$$XY = T^2$$
(where $X = s - t$ and $Y = s + t$, in units where $c=1$).

- **Lorentzian Symmetry**: The laws of physics are invariant under transformations that multiply $X$ by a constant and divide $Y$ by the same constant ($X \to kX, Y \to Y/k$). This is the "squashing and stretching" that preserves the hyperbolic area $XY$.
- **Relativity of Simultaneity**: Because these hyperbolas cross each other when observers move at different speeds, the chronological order of events can differ between observers.

## Significance to Free Will Theorem
This symmetry is the basis for the **Fin Axiom** (and its successor, the **Min Axiom**). It ensures that no information can be transmitted faster than light, as doing so would violate the causal structure of spacetime for some observers.
