# Causality and the Light-Speed Barrier

The prohibition of faster-than-light (FTL) communication is not just an arbitrary limit but a requirement for the consistency of causality in a relativistic universe.

## The Argument
In [[20260704120003-lorentzian-symmetry-hyperbolic-spacetime]], it is shown that different observers perceive the temporal order of events differently.

If an influence could travel faster than light:
1. There exists a reference frame where that influence travels "backward" in time.
2. This allows for causal loops (e.g., the "grandfather paradox"), where an effect precedes its cause and can potentially prevent its own cause from occurring.
3. This leads to a logical contradiction in the physical laws.

## The Fin/Min Axiom
Conway and Kochen's theorem relies on this principle. The **Fin Axiom** states that there is an upper bound on the speed of information. The **Min Axiom** refines this to say that experimenters can make choices independently of each other if they are separated such that light cannot travel between them in time.

## Significance
By assuming causality and Lorentzian invariance, the theorem constrains the possible ways a particle can "decide" its response, leading to the conclusion of particle agency if experimenter agency is assumed.
