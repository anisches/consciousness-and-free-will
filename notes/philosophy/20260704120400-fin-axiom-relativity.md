# FIN Axiom (Speed of Information)

The **FIN Axiom** (Finite Speed of Information) is based on the principles of Special Relativity and Lorentz Invariance.

## Definition
There exists a finite upper bound (the speed of light, $c$) to the speed at which information can be transmitted by any means.

## Application in the Free Will Theorem
In the context of the Conway-Kochen experiment:
- Experimenter A (on Earth) chooses a triple of orthogonal directions $(x, y, z)$.
- Experimenter B (on Mars) chooses a single direction $w$.
- The FIN axiom implies that the response of particle $a$ (on Earth) cannot depend on the choice made by Experimenter B (on Mars) if the events are spatially separated.
- Similarly, the response of particle $b$ (on Mars) cannot depend on the choice made by Experimenter A (on Earth).

## Relativistic Invariance
Conway emphasizes that because the time-ordering of spatially separated events is relative to the observer's coordinate frame, it is impossible to say that information is transmitted "instantaneously." In some frames, B's choice happens after A's measurement; in others, it happens before. Therefore, for the physics to be invariant, there can be no functional dependence on distant choices.
