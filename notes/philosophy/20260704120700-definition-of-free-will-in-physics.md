# Definition of Free Will in Physics

In the context of the **Free Will Theorem**, John Conway and Simon Kochen use a specific, technical definition of "free will."

## Technical Definition
An event (such as an experimenter's choice or a particle's response) is "free" if it is **not a function of the entire past history of the universe** in any given coordinate frame.

## Key Distinctions
- **Not Randomness**: Free will is not equated with randomness (though random events are also not functions of the past). The theorem focuses on the *negation* of functional determinism.
- **Invariance**: The definition must hold in all Lorentz-invariant coordinate frames. If a choice is determined in one frame, it is determined.
- **Independence from the Past**: To say a choice is free is to say that even if you knew everything that happened in the universe up until that moment, you could not calculate the outcome of that choice.

## Philosophical Stance
Conway argues that this is the same type of "freedom" we attribute to ourselves. If we believe that we are truly free to choose which button to press (and that our choice is not merely a play-out of initial conditions from the Big Bang), then we must grant that same level of independence to the particles we measure.
