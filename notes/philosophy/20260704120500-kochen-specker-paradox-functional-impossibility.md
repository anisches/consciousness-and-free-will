# Kochen-Specker Paradox (KS Paradox)

The **Kochen-Specker Paradox** (or Bell-Kochen-Specker theorem) is a mathematical proof showing that certain quantum mechanical results cannot be explained by "hidden variables" that are "non-contextual."

## The Core Finding
There is no mathematical function $f(d)$ that assigns a value (either $0$ or $1$) to every direction $d$ in 3D space such that for every set of three mutually perpendicular directions $\{x, y, z\}$, the values $\{f(x), f(y), f(z)\}$ are always a permutation of $\{1, 0, 1\}$.

## History
- Proved by Simon Kochen and Ernst Specker in 1967.
- John Conway uses a specific configuration of 33 directions (Peres configuration) to illustrate this impossibility.

## Role in the Free Will Theorem
The KS Paradox is the final "nail in the coffin" for deterministic (functional) interpretations of particle behavior in the Free Will Theorem. 
1. The proof assumes the particle responds according to a function of its past.
2. It shows that under the SPIN, TWIN, and FIN axioms, this function would have to be a function of direction only.
3. It further shows this function would have to satisfy the "101 law" (SPIN axiom).
4. The KS Paradox proves that no such function exists.
5. Therefore, the particle's response cannot be a function of its past.
