# TWIN Axiom (Entanglement)

The **TWIN Axiom** refers to the property of quantum entanglement between two particles in a specific state (often called the singleton state).

## Definition
If two spin-1 particles are prepared in a "singleton state" and then separated, they will yield identical answers if measured in the same direction. 
- If Experimenter A on Earth measures direction $w$ and gets response $r$, then Experimenter B on Mars measuring the same direction $w$ will also get response $r$.

## Significance
The TWIN axiom establishes a perfect correlation between distant particles. This correlation exists even though:
1. The response is not determined ahead of time (as shown by the SPIN axiom and KS paradox).
2. The particles may be spatially separated such that no signal can travel between them in the time between measurements.

## Use in the Free Will Theorem
In the proof of the Free Will Theorem, the TWIN axiom is used to link the functional responses of two distant particles. It allows the proof to "bridge" the measurements made by two different experimenters (A and B) and force a contradiction between their supposedly independent functional responses.
