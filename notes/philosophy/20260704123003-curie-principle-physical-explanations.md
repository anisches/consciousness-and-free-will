# Curie's Principle and Physical Explanations

**Curie's Principle** is a philosophical and scientific guideline used by John Conway to evaluate the validity of physical theories.

## Definition
The principle states that **an explanation of a set of facts must possess the same symmetries as the facts themselves.**

If the observed facts of the universe exhibit a certain symmetry (e.g., they are the same regardless of whether you are moving or standing still—Lorentz invariance), then any proposed mechanism or theory explaining those facts must also be invariant under that symmetry.

## Application in the Free Will Theorem
Conway applies Curie's Principle to reject "Janus-like" explanations for quantum entanglement:
- Certain models (like the **Janus Model**) can explain why entangled particles give the same results by assuming a hidden time-ordering.
- However, since there is no "preferred" time-ordering in relativity, and the experimental results are the same in all frames, a model that relies on a specific frame is not a valid explanation.
- This leads to the conclusion that the correlation between particles cannot be explained by a mechanistic, time-ordered process that respects relativity.

## Analogy: Solipsism
Conway compares non-symmetric explanations to **solipsism** (the belief that only one's own mind exists). Solipsism is "cheap" because it fails to respect the symmetry between different observers. Just as "I am the only real person" is an asymmetric explanation for a symmetric situation (where everyone could claim the same), non-relativistic explanations for relativistic facts are considered insufficient.
