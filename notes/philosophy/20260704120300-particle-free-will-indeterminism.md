# Particle Free Will as Indeterminism

In the context of the **Free Will Theorem**, John Conway uses the term "free will" in a specific, functional sense: a response that is **not a function of the past**.

## "Deciding on the Fly"
Conway argues that because there is no consistent set of pre-assigned values for squared spin (as shown by the [[20260704120100-kochen-specker-theorem-101-property|Kochen-Specker theorem]]), the particle must "make up its mind" at the moment of measurement.

> "The particle makes up its mind and tells you the answer on the spur of the moment... it doesn't have an answer in mind."

## Anthropomorphism vs. Indeterminacy
While the term "free will" is anthropomorphic, Conway uses it to highlight that the particle's behavior is just as "free" as the experimenter's. If the experimenter's choice of direction is not predetermined by the past, then by the logic of the theorem, neither is the particle's response.

## Emergence of Human Free Will
Conway suggests (though admits it is contentious) that the free will observed in humans might be an emergent property of the fundamental "free will" (indeterminacy) of the particles that compose us. He posits that biological mechanisms might amplify or coordinate these particle-level "decisions" rather than canceling them out.
