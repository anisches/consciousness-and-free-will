# Ineluctable Concepts and the Depth of Definitions

Deep concepts in philosophy and science, such as probability, randomness, and free will, are often "ineluctable"—meaning they cannot be defined in simpler terms without circularity.

John Conway, drawing on Robert Nozick's idea of "self-subsuming concepts," argues that this difficulty in definition is not a flaw but a symptom of the concept's depth. Fundamental properties cannot be reduced to more basic ones because they are the foundation.

For example:
- **Probability:** Most definitions of probability eventually rely on the concept of "likely" or "probable" outcomes.
- **Free Will:** Attempting to define free will often assumes a notion of choice or agency.

Rather than rejecting these concepts due to circularity, we should recognize that their ineluctability is a positive indicator of their status as fundamental building blocks of our understanding of the universe.
