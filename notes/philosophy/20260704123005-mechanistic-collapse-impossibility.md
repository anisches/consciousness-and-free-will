# Impossibility of Mechanistic Wavefunction Collapse

The Free Will Theorem has significant implications for the interpretation of quantum mechanics, specifically regarding the **collapse of the wavefunction**.

## Predictive Smearing vs. Definitive Reduction
In standard quantum theory:
- A particle's state evolves continuously and predictably according to the Schrödinger equation (**unitary development**). In this state, properties like position or spin components are often "smeared out."
- Upon measurement, the wavefunction **collapses** (or reduces) to a specific, definite value.

## The Theorem's Conclusion
John Conway argues that if the Free Will Theorem is true, then **there can be no mechanistic explanation for wavefunction collapse.** 

- If there were a mechanism that explained *why* a particle chooses a specific value during collapse, that mechanism would effectively be a function of the past (including any hidden variables or internal states).
- But the theorem proves that the particle's response is **not** a function of the past.
- Therefore, the collapse itself is an act of "freedom" by the particle that cannot be predicted or explained by any deterministic process.

## Scepticism of Conscious Observation
Conway is notably sceptical of the "New Age" view (once held by Eugene Wigner) that collapse requires a **conscious mind**. He acknowledges that the theorem proves the spin value does not exist *before* measurement, but measurement is simply a physical interaction. While measurement "triggers" the particle's choice, the choice itself is made by the particle, not by the consciousness of the observer.
