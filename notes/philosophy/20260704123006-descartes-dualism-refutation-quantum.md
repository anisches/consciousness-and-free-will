# Refutation of Cartesian Dualism via Quantum Indeterminacy

John Conway uses the Free Will Theorem to challenge the historical dualism proposed by **René Descartes**.

## Cartesian Dualism
Descartes famously held a dualistic view of the world:
- **Human Minds**: Possess free will and are not constrained by the laws of physics.
- **Physical World**: Entirely deterministic and "robotic." Even the human body (minus the mind) was considered a machine.

## The Theorem's Challenge
The Free Will Theorem demonstrates that this duality is logically inconsistent with the observed behavior of matter (specifically the SPIN and TWIN axioms) and the assumption of human freedom.

- If we assume humans have free will (as Descartes did), then the theorem shows that the particles they interact with **must also have free will.**
- It is impossible to have a "free" mind interacting with an "entirely deterministic" physical world if that physical world obeys the laws of quantum mechanics and relativity.
- Therefore, the "robotic" nature of the physical world outside of minds is disproved. 

## Leibniz and the Principle of Sufficient Reason
Similarly, the theorem refutes **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz's Principle of Sufficient Reason**, which states that nothing happens without a reason. In the case of a particle's choice during an experiment, there is no "reason" (no cause in the past) why it chose one result over another. The choice is fundamental and undetermined.
