# Free Will as an Explanatory Theory for Quantum Indeterminacy

The assumption of human free will provides a powerful explanatory framework for why quantum mechanics is incomplete and how agency emerges in the universe.

Conway applies a "surprised" argument:
1. **Assumption (P):** Humans have free will (ability to choose experiments independently of the past).
2. **Deduction (Q):** Particles must also possess a form of free will (Free Will Theorem).
3. **Observation:** Quantum mechanics is indeed incomplete and non-deterministic.

Because the surprising deduction (Q) aligns with observed reality, it serves as evidence for the initial assumption (P). 

Furthermore, this framework explains:
- **Origin of Agency:** Our own free will can be seen as emerging from the sampling of "free" particle decisions within the brain.
- **Physics Incompleteness:** No correct theory can ever be complete because the particles themselves have not "decided" their responses until the moment of measurement.

In contrast, determinism explains nothing—it simply asserts that things are the way they are without accounting for the appearance of choice or the specific nature of quantum behavior.
