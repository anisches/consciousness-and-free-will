# The Modern Lack of Evidence for Determinism

Despite the widespread belief in determinism within philosophy and biological sciences, there is currently no physical evidence to support it.

Historically, the success of Newtonian mechanics—which was deterministic and time-reversible—provided a strong foundation for a deterministic worldview (Laplace's Demon). However, the advent of quantum mechanics in the early 20th century removed this empirical basis by introducing fundamental indeterminacy.

John Conway points out:
- **Newtonian Dynamics:** Superseded by quantum mechanics.
- **Consistency vs. Truth:** While determinism is logically consistent (e.g., replaying the universe), consistency does not prove it is true.
- **The "Necessary Illusion":** Many determinists (like Dennis Overbye) admit they must live as if they have free will, labeling it a "necessary illusion," yet they lack a scientific reason for why they believe determinism is true in the first place.

Conway argues that the burden of proof should shift: if one disbelieves in free will, they must provide evidence for the determinism they assert, which has been absent for nearly a century of physics.
