# Pragmatic and Observational Arguments for Free Will

John Conway presents two non-deductive arguments for free will based on scientific practice and everyday observation:

### 1. The Pragmatic Argument from Science
Scientific experimentation assumes that the researcher is free to choose which experiment to perform. If the experimenter lacks free will, nature could be in a "conspiracy," forcing the experimenter to only perform tests that confirm a certain (possibly false) theory. 
- Science relies on the counterfactual: "I chose to press the left button, but I *could have* pressed the right one." 
- If this assumption is false, the logical justification for all scientific assertions becomes questionable.

### 2. The Observational Argument from Time Reversibility
While many fundamental physical laws (Newtonian, Maxwellian) are mathematically time-reversible, the world we observe is decidedly not.
- **Example:** A movie of a cat run backwards looks entirely different from the movie run forward.
- Conway argues that if one believes the universe is truly time-reversible, they must explain why reality appears so fundamentally one-directional. This one-directionality is linked to the "collapse" of possibilities and the making of free decisions.
