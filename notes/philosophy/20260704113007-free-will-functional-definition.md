# Functional Definition of Free Will (Conway-Kochen)

In the Free Will Theorem, John Conway and Simon Kochen use a precise, "functional" definition of free will that avoids metaphysical or moral baggage.

## Definition
An entity (whether an experimenter or a particle) has **free will** if its response or choice is **not a function of the past history of the universe**.

## Key Distinctions
- **Not Randomness**: Conway distinguishes this from mere randomness. While a random number generator might not be a function of the past, the "free" choice in the theorem is tied to the agency of the experimenter.
- **No Consciousness Required**: The theorem does not claim that elementary particles are "conscious" or "aware." It only claims they possess the same property of *indeterminacy relative to the past* that we attribute to ourselves when we claim to have free will.
- **No Moral Responsibility**: The definition is not concerned with whether an agent *should* be held responsible for their actions, only with whether those actions were pre-determined.

## Why this definition?
By using this minimal definition, Conway and Kochen can construct a rigorous logical and mathematical proof that links human agency to the fundamental behavior of matter.
