## Idea
The claim is that free will is not merely a conceptual error about how the world works (determinism vs. indeterminism), but that the *subjective experience* of having free will is itself non-existent when one pays close attention to the mind. Harris argues that while we *think* we feel like authors of our thoughts, the actual phenomenology of a thought's arrival is spontaneous and mysterious.

## Why it matters
If the experience of free will doesn't actually exist, then the common argument that "we must act as if we have free will because we feel it" loses its empirical footing.
