# Conway and Kochen's Free Will Theorem - Lecture 2: Scientific Consequences

## Overview
In the second lecture of the Princeton series, John Conway discusses the scientific and philosophical implications of the Free Will Theorem. He addresses historical philosophical positions, the nature of randomness vs. freedom, and the relativistic constraints on physical explanations.

## Key Topics
- **Disproof of Historical Dualism**:
    - **Descartes**: The theorem disproves the duality where minds are free but the physical world is robotic. If humans are free, particles must be too. [[20260704123006-descartes-dualism-refutation-quantum]]
    - **Leibniz**: Challenges the **Principle of Sufficient Reason**. For particles, there is no "reason" in the past history that dictates their specific response to an experiment. [[20260704120400-disproof-principle-sufficient-reason-quantum]]
- **The Janus Model and Curie's Principle**:
    - Introduces a "Hexagonal Universe" toy model.
    - The **Janus Model** is an explanation that assumes an external operator (Janus) who knows everything, but it violates the symmetry of the physical facts.
    - **Curie's Principle**: A theory that does not respect the symmetry of the facts it explains cannot be the true explanation. [[20260704123003-curie-principle-physical-explanations]]
- **Freedom vs. Randomness**:
    - Conway distinguishes between "random" (can be determined in advance, like a table of random numbers) and "free" (cannot be determined in advance due to non-local constraints). [[20260704123001-particle-freedom-vs-randomness]]
- **Mechanism of Wavefunction Collapse**:
    - The theorem implies that there can be no mechanistic explanation for the collapse of the wavefunction. [[20260704123005-mechanistic-collapse-impossibility]]
- **Free State Assumption**:
    - Replaces the nebulous "human free will" with the more standard physical requirement that initial states can be essentially arbitrary (the "Free State" assumption). [[20260704123004-free-state-assumption-initial-conditions]]
- **Hidden Variable Theories**:
    - No hidden variable theory can be both relativistic and deterministic.
- **Second Time Around Argument**:
    - Reiterates that free will cannot be proven by pure deduction, as a deterministic "replay" would be indistinguishable from the first "free" run. [[20260704113008-second-time-around-argument]]
