# Kochen-Specker Paradox

The **Kochen-Specker Theorem** (or Paradox) is a mathematical proof showing that no set of "hidden variables" can pre-determine the outcomes of quantum measurements while remaining consistent with the predictions of quantum mechanics.

## The Core Problem
The paradox specifically addresses the measurement of squared spin for spin-1 particles. It proves that there is no way to assign a value (0 or 1) to every possible measurement direction in 3D space such that for *any* three mutually perpendicular directions, exactly two are assigned '1' and one is assigned '0' (the **SPIN** axiom).

## Practical Reduction
While the original proof involved 117 directions, it has been reduced by Asher Peres and others to a set of 33 directions. There is no consistent "coloring" of these 33 directions with 0s and 1s that satisfies the 101 Rule.

## Implication
Since the particle *does* give consistent answers (satisfying the 101 Rule) when measured, the answers cannot have existed before the measurement was made. The particle must "choose" its answer on the fly.
