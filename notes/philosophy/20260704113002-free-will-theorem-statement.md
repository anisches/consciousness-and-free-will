# The Free Will Theorem (Conway-Kochen)

The **Free Will Theorem** states that if the choice of measurement settings by human experimenters is not a function of the past (i.e., they have "free will"), then the responses of the elementary particles being measured are also not a function of the past.

## Logic
1. Assume experimenters are "free" to choose measurement directions (e.g., choosing one of 33 buttons).
2. The **SPIN** axiom shows that particle responses cannot be pre-determined for all directions.
3. The **TWIN** and **FIN** axioms show that particles cannot "communicate" on the fly to coordinate their answers if they are remotely separated.
4. Therefore, if the experimenter's choice is truly free, the particle's response must be generated "on the fly" in a way that is not determined by the history of the universe.

## Implications
The theorem suggests that free will is not a high-level emergent property of biological brains but a fundamental property of matter itself. If we have it, particles have it too.
