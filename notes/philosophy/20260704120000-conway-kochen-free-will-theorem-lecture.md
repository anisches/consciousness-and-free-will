# Conway and Kochen's Free Will Theorem - Lecture 1

## Overview
This lecture by John Conway introduces the foundations of the Free Will Theorem, developed with Simon Kochen. The theorem posits that if humans have free will (in the sense that their choices are not determined by the past history of the universe), then elementary particles also possess a form of free will.

## Key Axioms
1. **Spin Axiom (SPIN)**: For a spin-1 particle, the squared components of spin in three mutually perpendicular directions are always a permutation of $(1, 1, 0)$. This is an operational property.
2. **Fin Axiom (FIN / MIN)**: Information cannot be transmitted faster than light. Later refined to the **Min Axiom**, which focuses on the independence of observers and the speed of light barrier.
3. **Twin Axiom (TWIN)**: If two particles are prepared in a "twinned" state (entangled) and then separated, they will give the same response to the same experiment performed in the same direction.

## Key Concepts
- **Kochen-Specker Paradox**: Shows that it is impossible to assign a value to every direction such that the 101-rule is satisfied. This implies that the spin value does not exist as a function of direction prior to measurement.
- **Lorentzian Symmetry**: Conway explains special relativity using a hyperbolic model ($XY = T^2$) where time is not absolute but depends on the observer's motion.
- **Causality**: Faster-than-light communication would lead to causal paradoxes (e.g., killing your own grandfather) because it implies backward time travel in some reference frames.
- **EPR Paradox**: The "Twin Axiom" is based on the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen experiment, demonstrating non-local correlations that don't violate relativity if no information is sent.

## Core Argument
The theorem argues that the response of a particle to an experiment is not a function of the entire past history of the universe. If it were, and if we allow for the free choice of the experimenter, we reach a contradiction with the axioms.
