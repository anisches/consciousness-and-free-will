# The "Second Time Around" Argument

John Conway uses the **"Second Time Around"** argument to explain why determinism can never be strictly disproved by logic or science, even if it is not "true."

## The Analogy
Imagine watching a movie for the first time; it seems full of suspense and undetermined choices. Now imagine watching it a second time. Every event is exactly the same, and from your perspective, it is entirely deterministic. However, the characters *within* the movie still "experience" their choices as they did the first time.

## The Philosophical Point
The universe could be a "second performance" (or any subsequent performance) of a pre-recorded script. If the initial "run" involved free choices, a subsequent "replay" would be identical in every detail but would be strictly deterministic.

## Consistency vs. Truth
Determinism is **logically consistent** with all observations. One can always claim that every "free" choice was actually written in the "big book" of the universe from the start. However, Conway argues that while we cannot disprove the *consistency* of determinism, we have no reason to believe it is *true*, especially given the logical consequences for the behavior of matter (as shown in the Free Will Theorem).
