# History of the Problem

## Primitive Timeline

### Ancient Framing

Early questions about action focus less on modern metaphysical freedom and more on responsibility, virtue, compulsion, ignorance, habit, and rational control.

Initial anchor:

- Aristotle: voluntary and involuntary action, choice, responsibility.

### Theological and Moral Framing

The problem sharpens when human responsibility must coexist with divine foreknowledge, grace, sin, and providence.

Initial anchors:

- Augustine
- Aquinas

### Early Modern Mind and Causation

The rise of mechanistic science changes the pressure on freedom. If nature is law-governed, what kind of thing is willing?

Initial anchors:

- Descartes
- Hobbes
- Spinoza
- Leibniz
- Hume

### Enlightenment and Practical Reason

Freedom becomes tied to autonomy, rationality, morality, and law-like agency.

Initial anchor:

- Kant

### Scientific and Psychological Pressure

The debate absorbs psychology, unconscious processing, evolution, behaviorism, cognitive science, and neuroscience.

Initial anchors:

- William James
- Freud
- behaviorism
- cognitive science
- Libet

### Contemporary Split

Modern debates often separate:

- metaphysical free will
- moral responsibility
- conscious intention
- sense of agency
- neural initiation of action
- decision-making under uncertainty

## Working Rule

Do not treat "free will" as one question. Most confusion comes from mixing at least four questions:

1. Could I have done otherwise?
2. Am I the source of my action?
3. Am I morally responsible?
4. Was my conscious intention causally important?
