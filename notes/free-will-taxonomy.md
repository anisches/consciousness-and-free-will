# Free Will Taxonomy

## Core Positions

### Libertarian Free Will

Free will exists and is incompatible with determinism. Human agents have a special kind of freedom not reducible to deterministic causation.

### Hard Determinism

Determinism is true and free will does not exist.

### Compatibilism

Free will is compatible with determinism. Freedom is usually understood in terms of acting according to one's reasons, desires, values, or higher-order endorsements without coercion.

### Hard Incompatibilism

Free will is incompatible with both determinism and indeterminism, so free will is unavailable either way.

### Revisionism

Ordinary free-will concepts are partly confused, but can be revised into something defensible and useful.

### Illusionism / Skepticism

Free will, at least in the ordinary robust sense, is an illusion or not supported by our best theory of mind and action.

## Axes To Track

- alternatives: could the agent have done otherwise?
- sourcehood: is the agent the true source of the act?
- control: did the agent control the action?
- reasons-responsiveness: did the agent respond to reasons?
- consciousness: did conscious intention matter?
- responsibility: is blame or praise justified?
- punishment: what follows socially or legally?
