## Idea
# Quantifying Perceptual Sampling Drift

## Wasserstein Distance and Fixation Maps
Similarity between fixation density maps (how an observer samples a scene) can be quantified using the **Wasserstein distance** (Earth Mover's Distance). This metric captures the "cost" of transforming one sampling distribution into another.

## Longitudinal Decay of Sampling Similarity
Empirical data show that the Wasserstein distance between fixation maps increases linearly (or systematically) with the number of sessions separating the observations.
- **Short-term (within session)**: High similarity/low distance.
- **Long-term (weeks)**: Increasing dissimilarity.

## Systematic vs. Random Noise
The drift in sampling is not random noise; it represents a systematic shift in how naturalistic visual scenes are explored over time. This shift is sufficient to alter the effective input to the visual system even when the external stimulus is static.
