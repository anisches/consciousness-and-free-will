## Idea
# Satellite Glial Cells Drive Homeostatic Synaptic Structural Plasticity in Sympathetic Neurons

**Authors:** Joshua Harrison, Ellie Greene, Aaron Yang, Jumana Akoad, Laurie Chen, Rui Gong, Xianghan Liu, Susan Birren
**DOI:** 10.64898/2026.05.10.723591
**Date:** 2026-5-13

## Summary
Harrison et al. (2026) report a novel homeostatic plasticity mechanism in the peripheral nervous system (PNS). They show that sympathetic neurons (SNs) bidirectional modulate their synaptic density in response to long-term activity changes. This process is driven by satellite glial cells (SGCs), which sense cholinergic activity and downregulate NGF and TNFα to stabilize circuit output. In spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHR), this glial-mediated homeostatic mechanism is attenuated, potentially contributing to pathological SN hyperactivity.

## Key Findings
- **Bidirectional Homeostasis:** SNs adjust synaptic density to counteract long-term activity shifts.
- **Glial Requirement:** SGCs are necessary and sufficient for this plasticity.
- **Molecular Pathway:** Cholinergic signaling -> SGCs -> NGF/TNFα downregulation.
- **Disease Relevance:** SHRs show attenuated glial response, linking plasticity defects to hypertension.
