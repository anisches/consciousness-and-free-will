## Idea
# Non-Selective Manifold Informative Neurons

A key finding from the application of the [[DRIADA toolkit]] (Pospelov et al., 2026) is the existence of a large population of neurons that are **highly informative about the leading dimensions of a [[neural manifold]] but are not selective for any measured behavioral features.**

## The Finding

In hippocampal datasets where mice navigated an open field:
- Researchers measured 11 behavioral features (position, velocity, etc.).
- They found that **~57% of neurons** that carried high mutual information with the manifold's principal dimensions were "non-selective" (i.e., they lacked significant tuning to the 11 measured variables).

## Significance

This discovery challenges the "tuning curve" paradigm of neuroscience, where neurons are primarily understood through their selectivity to external stimuli or motor outputs. 

1. **Latent Coding**: It suggests that the manifold structure is a global population property that does not require every participant neuron to be a "feature detector."
2. **The "Dark Matter" of the Brain**: These neurons contribute to the stability and dimensionality of the representation but remain "silent" or "noise-like" when analyzed through the lens of simple behavioral correlation.
3. **Multi-Dimensional State-Spaces**: These neurons might be selective for unmeasured variables—internal states, intentions, history-dependent variables, or abstract "agency" signals—that are not captured by standard behavioral tracking.

## Relationship to Agency

If the majority of manifold-informative neurons are "non-selective," it implies that the internal model of the world (the manifold) is much richer than the behavioral tracking suggests. The "freedom" or "indeterminacy" observed in behavior may actually be high-dimensional trajectories through these unmeasured latent spaces, which are only visible when looking at the global manifold structure rather than single-cell tuning.
