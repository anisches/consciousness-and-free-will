## Idea
# Libet, Schurger, and Veto Models of Intention

## Core thesis

Libet-style experiments show readiness potentials preceding reported conscious intention, but this does not straightforwardly prove that "the brain decides before you do." Schurger's accumulator model reinterprets the readiness potential as spontaneous activity accumulating toward threshold, while veto/inhibition work reframes agency as gating, cancellation, and self-control.

## Key distinctions

- **Readiness potential:** averaged neural buildup before movement.
- **Accumulator model:** spontaneous fluctuations cross a threshold for action.
- **Veto / free won't:** late-stage inhibition can cancel or suppress an action.
- **Limitation:** arbitrary finger movements are not the same as deliberative moral choices.

## Why it matters for agency

The branch shifts the question from "who starts the action?" to "what architecture controls initiation, suppression, timing, and policy selection?"
