## Idea
# Representational geometry as a fidelity metric for connectome-constrained networks: evidence from the Drosophila visual system

- **Authors**: Michael G. Zhou, Jennifer O. Hasler
- **Journal/Preprint**: bioRxiv (2026)
- **DOI**: 10.64898/2026.06.10.731214

## Summary
What does biological wiring contribute to computation? Behavioral fidelity (outputs) is insufficient to verify biological fidelity (internal representations), as arbitrary models can be trained to replicate behavior (e.g., C. elegans model walking like a fly). 

Zhou & Hasler propose **representational geometry** — the structure of pairwise distances between population responses — as a metric for biological fidelity. They apply RSA and CKA to the **Flyvis ensemble** (connectome-constrained Drosophila visual system) vs. random baselines.

### Key Results
1. **Circular Direction Geometry**: Connectome-constrained networks produce smooth circular direction representations that random networks (even those with dynamic stability constraints) cannot replicate.
2. **Biological Alignment**: The representational geometry of connectome-constrained networks matches biological T4/T5 direction tuning significantly better than random baselines (r=0.930 vs. r=0.603).
3. **Fidelity Metric**: Representational geometry discriminates real biological wiring from arbitrary wiring without requiring a behavioral decoder.
