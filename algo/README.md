# Algorithmic Free Will & Machine Consciousness

Computational frameworks for the mind as an engine of logic, control, prediction, inhibition, and recursive self-modeling. This branch rejects the cartoon contrast between "free soul" and "dead machine": the live question is whether algorithmic systems can become **agents** when they model consequences, suppress impulses, allocate attention, and choose policies under uncertainty.

## Core Branches

### Accumulator Models of Intention
*Self-initiated action may emerge when spontaneous neural activity crosses a decision threshold.* Schurger's accumulator model complicates the old Libet story: readiness potentials may not be a hidden decision already made, but pre-decision fluctuations biased toward threshold.

### The Veto Layer / Intentional Inhibition
*Free will may be less "first spark" than last gate.* Libet's own "free won't" idea, and Brass & Haggard's work on intentional inhibition, frame agency as cancellation, suppression, and policy gating — a control layer over impulses.

### Active Inference as Algorithmic Agency
*Organisms can be modeled as prediction-error-minimizing controllers selecting policies under uncertainty.* Active inference and predictive processing convert agency into a loop: perception updates the model; action samples the world to fulfill or revise predictions.

### Bounded Rational Agents
*Real choice is not omniscient optimization; it is satisficing under limited compute, time, and information.* Simon-style bounded rationality is a better computational model for human agency than perfect utility maximization.

### Sandboxed Self-Models
*Consciousness as a secure, isolated workspace running counterfactual simulations before action.* This is useful as a metaphor for deliberation, but it must avoid smuggling in a homunculus: the "sandbox" itself must be implemented by attention, working memory, affect, and control circuits.

### Infinite Loops and Rumination
*Mathematical similarities between program loops and anxiety/obsessive thought patterns.* Rumination becomes the pathological edge of recursive self-modeling: simulation without policy resolution.
